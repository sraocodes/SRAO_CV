About

Sathyanarayan Rao

I am a computational scientist in Bengaluru. For nineteen years I have been building models of systems that resist direct measurement, and working out what you can honestly infer from the traces they leave in a sensor.

It started with plasma. At Huntsville I extended a particle-in-cell code from two dimensions to three and learned to wait overnight for a cluster to tell me whether I had got a boundary condition right. Then light — laser-induced photodegradation in an Alabama cleanroom, then Maxwell–Bloch solvers in Paderborn for light moving through excitonic media.

The PhD in Belgium was the turn that mattered. The question was whether you could see a plant's roots without digging them up, by reading the electrical field of the soil around them. Answering it meant coupling root architecture, water uptake and geoelectrical forward models into a single simulation, then taking the whole apparatus into a field of grasses under drought and seeing whether it survived contact with reality. Mostly it did.

At Jülich the systems got bigger and the problem changed shape: less about writing a model than about making independently developed scientific codes — C, C++, Fortran, Python — exchange state without lying to each other. At IISc the scale moved again, to catchments and satellites and decade-long records of how soil in semi-arid Karnataka holds water.

Now a third thread runs alongside. I design scientific benchmarks that test whether frontier AI systems reason or merely recall — which is, structurally, the same inverse problem I have been solving all along. You cannot open the system. You can only choose your probes carefully and be honest about what the answer supports.

Outside that: I write. Two books in 2026, one about traffic and one about three days at a cremation ghat. I make Compute Stories on YouTube, sit on the Young Hydrologic Society blog committee, photograph the cities I have lived in, and ride a modest motorcycle through Bengaluru traffic with more anxiety than the machine deserves.
