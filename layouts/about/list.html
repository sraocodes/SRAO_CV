About

Sathyanarayan Rao

I'm a computational scientist. Most of my work has been building models of physical systems and working out what you can infer about them from measurements.

I started in plasma physics at UA Huntsville, on a particle-in-cell code — extending it from 2D electrostatic to 3D electromagnetic and parallelising it with MPI. Then some experimental optics at Alabama A&M, and after that Fortran solvers for the Maxwell–Bloch equations at Paderborn.

My PhD at UCLouvain was on geoelectrical sensing of plant roots: how much you can say about root structure and water uptake from electrical resistivity measurements. That meant coupling root architecture models, water uptake models and electrical forward models, and then running field and lab ERT campaigns to compare the simulations against measurements.

At Jülich I worked on coupling crop models with root models. Mostly interface work between codes written in C, C++, Fortran and Python, plus containers so other people could actually run them.

Now I'm a research associate at IISc, working on soil moisture retrieval from Sentinel-1, fusing it with SMAP for longer records, and evaluating watershed interventions in Karnataka. Alongside that I do scientific AI evaluation — designing benchmarks that test whether frontier models actually work through a physics or maths problem or just pattern-match their way to an answer. I also co-founded Chakradhari Computational Technologies, a small scientific R&D company.

The common thread, if there is one, is computational modelling and inference from measurements. The subject matter has changed a lot; that part hasn't.

Outside research I write — two books in 2026 — make videos on Compute Stories, sit on the Young Hydrologic Society blog committee, take photographs, and ride a motorcycle.
